# Product

## Register

product

## Users

OpenHabit is for people who want to track habits but have bounced off streak-driven trackers. They may use it from Telegram or a browser, often in a low-energy moment when they need the app to help them return rather than make them feel behind. Users value calm feedback, flexible schedules, private data, and a tool that can stay useful across missed days, imperfect weeks, and long-term progress.

## Product Purpose

OpenHabit is an open-source anti-streak habit tracker built around returning, not perfection. It helps people record habits, keep notes, use gentle reminders, review activity over time, and get optional AI reflections without turning a missed day into a reset. Success means the interface keeps the door open: users can come back after a day, a week, or a month and still understand what matters next.

## Brand Personality

Calm, forgiving, and open. The voice should feel like a steady companion rather than a coach with a scoreboard. It is practical and honest, but intentionally gentle: no hype, no shame, no inflated productivity promises. The open-source posture should feel transparent and trustworthy, not technical for its own sake.

## Anti-references

OpenHabit should not look or sound like a streak-pressure app: avoid fire streaks, punishment loops, guilt copy, reset anxiety, and achievement addiction. Avoid generic SaaS landing-page patterns such as hero metric templates, gradient hype, repeated feature-card grids, and corporate productivity language. Avoid gamified dopamine UI: badges, confetti-first feedback, casino-bright colors, and over-rewarding simple actions should not define the experience.

## Design Principles

- Make returning feel normal: every surface should reassure users that missed days are part of the story, not a failure state.
- Show progress as context, not judgment: visualizations should reveal patterns without scoring the person behind them.
- Keep the product quiet enough to trust: interactions should be familiar, readable, and restrained, saving delight for meaningful moments.
- Be open by design: privacy, portability, self-hosting, and source availability should feel visible and credible.
- Respect real-life variation: schedules, notes, reminders, languages, themes, and accessibility needs should support imperfect daily use.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Preserve strong keyboard support, visible focus states, readable contrast in light and dark themes, reduced-motion alternatives, RTL support, and the existing multi-language experience. State must never rely on color alone, and calm motion should remain optional for users who request reduced motion.
